\section{Concept}

The game itself is played in 2 repeating phases:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item{the decisions phase,}
	\item{the simulation phase}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decisions phase}

During the decisions phase you act as the board of the company, with absolute
control over your company.

The players all start out with a shell of a company and a pittance of money. You
then design your product (e.g.: a coffee machine), choosing parts and
determining the properties of said product, ideally, carving out a niche for
your company. The players must make sure to effectively market your product so that the
population actually know that it exists and can thus buy it.

In later stages of the game the players are finally able to afford high tech
production equipment and manufacturing techniques. This way can develop their
products and market them so that they cover large sections of the market more
effectively and efficiently. For example by purchasing machines that produce
products quicker, which, however, are also far more expensive than the standard
ones. Companies can also choose to offer multiple products at once allowing them
to cover multiple segments of the market.

In the end players lose when either
\begin{enumerate}
	\item{Their company goes bankrupt.}
	\item{Another player gets a controlling share in their company: at which point
	      the companies are merged (under the control of the bigger company), causing
	      the smaller company's former owner to lose.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Simulation phase}

After each company has submitted their decisions ending the decisions phase, the
economic simulation runs, calculating whether employees at the company are
satisfied enough to actually stay at the company, computing the production of
products, running of the company (based on player decisions) and, finally,
simulating the marketing and purchasing of products, determining whether the
company was successful in its job.
